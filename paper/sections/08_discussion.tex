\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

We began with a question that comes before the usual implementation story: when does a substrate earn a logic layer? The answer developed through Definition~\ref{def:logic-layer} asks for more than labels \(0\) and \(1\). It asks for packaged predicates that persist, induced operators that close on them, and an audit that measures mismatch and loss. In this sense logic is not primitive; it is earned by closure. The four results each sharpen a different part of that answer.

\paragraph{Parity is a measurement, not a mystique.}
In the parity-sector laboratory the parity lens has route mismatch exactly zero at every leakage rate and horizon, whereas the other two balanced partitions and the AND predicate have strictly positive mismatch at every tested point, with explicit closed forms (Proposition~\ref{prop:parity-exact}). XOR does not occupy a privileged metaphysical tier. Parity is the distinction that this laboratory's sector structure respects, and the diagnostics detect that. A substrate can make some logical forms cheap and exact without making every familiar Boolean predicate equally natural. To ``XOR a stone'' is to find that parity can be the first logical distinction a substrate stabilizes.

\paragraph{Gate closure is a regime, not a verdict.}
NOT and AND close in short-horizon, high-barrier regimes and degrade over longer horizons under weaker staging, even without output noise, because their stored inputs drift. CNOT shows a different failure: a noiseless one-step CNOT is a perfect input--output channel while its one-bit output lens is maximally unclosed, and at even horizons the same lens becomes exactly closed again. A perfect input--output channel is not the same thing as a closed state description. Different gates fail in different ways because closure depends on the carrier the layer retains, not only on noise.

\paragraph{Forgetting side information changes the logical object.}
The retained and erased views of one CNOT laboratory differ in route mismatch and worst-case closure defect by \(0.96\) and in unretained input information by a factor of \(8.07\). That is not well described as ``the same gate with a larger bill.'' It changes what counts as the state of the layer. Packaging and accounting are inseparable here: packaging fixes the carrier, and the audit shows that discarding the control turns a closed reversible operator into a many-to-one report with very different closure properties \cite{bennett1982thermodynamics,parrondo2015thermoinfo,esposito2012coarsegraining,kawaguchi2013hiddenepr}. The micro readout makes a complementary point: describing the output more finely adds entropy but no information about the input, so entropy drop alone does not measure what a description has lost.

\paragraph{The framework runs in both directions.}
The diagnostics that test closure also discover it. The pipeline recovers the intended stable bits exactly in both benchmark laboratories, and it reconstructs a NOT gate whose exact channel error equals the laboratory's output noise. Because each partition's orientation is a convention, the recovered gate is NOT in declared coordinates; what is discovered without convention is the pair of carriers and the nonconstant channel between them. The pipeline starts from transition structure, not from declared variables, so it offers a way to ask from the dynamics alone which distinctions deserve logical status and which operators act on them.

\paragraph{Scope and limitations.}
The laboratories are finite Markov chains chosen for diagnostic clarity, not for physical realism, and the results are demonstrations within them rather than claims about natural substrates. Definition~\ref{def:logic-layer} is an operational criterion with application-dependent tolerances; we report the diagnostics instead of a single binary verdict on whether a system ``has'' a logic layer, and specifying thresholds would be necessary for such a verdict. We do not implement an independent solver for feasibility constraints. Route mismatch is an average over a chosen weighting, and a single-probe closure defect can vanish by cancellation; we therefore pair the probe with the worst-case defect where it matters. Apparent entropy production is computed per evaluation window and audits a Markov approximation; it is not a measurement of dissipated heat. The discovery pipeline is limited to binary partitions and one-input gates and relies on finite-precision heuristics for behavioral classes and repeated eigenvalues. Finally, in the set-based laboratories studied here the expressible events always form a Boolean algebra, so the paper does not exhibit non-Boolean event semantics; it measures which Boolean operations a layer can hold stable and execute. For partition-based routes from classical state spaces to complementary observables and non-Boolean propositional structure, see \cite{ellerman2010partitions,atmanspacher2015nonboolean}; for the quantum-logical background, see \cite{birkhoff1936quantumlogic}; and for a Six Birds extension in that direction, see \cite{tsiokos2026quantum}.

\paragraph{Outlook.}
The broader consequence is pluralistic. The question is not whether logic is ``really there'' behind every coarse description, but which closures a layer can sustain, at which horizon, on which carrier, and at what audit cost. A layer may hold a parity bit exactly while conjunction leaks, may support a reversible operator only on a larger carrier than the one being read, or may express a complement it cannot execute. The Six Birds viewpoint does not eliminate the diversity of logics; it ties each to the closures a substrate can actually sustain. If XOR teaches anything, it is not that parity is magical but that substrates tell us which distinctions deserve logical status. Logic is not what a theorist can name at a layer; it is what a substrate can stabilize, close, and audit there.
