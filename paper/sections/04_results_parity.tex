\section{Parity Robustness}
\label{sec:results-parity}

The parity-sector laboratory is built around leakage between the even-parity states \(\{00,11\}\) and the odd-parity states \(\{01,10\}\) of a two-bit register, so the natural candidate coarse variable is the parity bit \(a \oplus b\). We compare it with two kinds of alternatives on the same four register values: the familiar Boolean predicate \(a \wedge b\), and balanced binary partitions, which split the four values into two pairs. The comparison is about closure rather than prediction: the measured quantity is route mismatch, so smaller is better.

Up to swapping the two labels there are only three balanced partitions: parity, the first bit \(a\), and the second bit \(b\). Each appears twice among the six labelings that assign two values to each label. The frozen experiment draws \(60\) such labelings at random with replacement and reports their median RM, so the ``random'' baseline in fact samples these three partitions. Because the family is this small, the comparison can be made exactly.

\begin{proposition}[Exact route mismatch in the parity-sector laboratory]
\label{prop:parity-exact}
Let \(p=p_{\mathrm{leak}}\in[0,1]\), let \(\tau\ge 1\), and use uniform weights. Then
\[
\mathrm{RM}_\tau(a\oplus b)=0,\qquad
\mathrm{RM}_\tau(a)=\frac{p}{2}\,|1-2p|^{\tau-1},\qquad
\mathrm{RM}_\tau(b)=\frac{p}{10}\,|1-2p|^{\tau-1},
\]
\[
\mathrm{RM}_\tau(a\wedge b)=\frac{2}{3}\,\Bigl|\frac12-\frac{6p}{5}\Bigr|\,|1-2p|^{\tau-1},
\qquad
S_\tau(a\oplus b;\mu)=\frac{1+(1-2p)^{\tau}}{2}\ \text{ for every } \mu .
\]
Consequently, for \(0<p<\tfrac12\) the median RM over the six balanced labelings equals \(\mathrm{RM}_\tau(b)>0\), whereas the parity lens has RM exactly zero at every horizon.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every microstate's transition row depends only on the parity of its register value: rows of the two even values coincide, as do rows of the two odd values, and all \(d\) copies of a value share its row. Write \(r_E\) and \(r_O\) for the even and odd rows. The same holds for \(P^\tau\), whose rows are \(r_E P^{\tau-1}\) and \(r_O P^{\tau-1}\). Hence each parity fiber contains a single profile and \(\mathrm{RM}_\tau(a\oplus b)=0\). Since the parity flips with probability \(p\) at each step independently of everything else, it is unchanged after \(\tau\) steps with probability \([1+(1-2p)^\tau]/2\), whatever the starting state. For the other lenses, let \(\delta=r_E-r_O\). The total mass of \(\delta\) on the even sector is \((1-p)-p=1-2p\) and on the odd sector is \(-(1-2p)\); because \(P\) has row \(r_E\) on even states and \(r_O\) on odd states, \(\delta P=(1-2p)\,\delta\), and so the even and odd rows of \(P^\tau\) differ by \((1-2p)^{\tau-1}\delta\). The lenses \(a\) and \(b\) put one even and one odd value in each fiber, so at horizon \(\tau\) each profile lies at distance \(\tfrac12|1-2p|^{\tau-1}\) times the \(\ell_1\) norm of the pushed-forward \(\delta\) from the fiber average. Pushing \(\delta\) forward gives \((\tfrac p2,-\tfrac p2)\) through \(a\) and \((-\tfrac p{10},\tfrac p{10})\) through \(b\), which yields the stated values. For \(a\wedge b\), the fiber \(\{11\}\) is a single value and contributes nothing, while the fiber \(\{00,01,10\}\), which carries three quarters of the weight, contains one even and two odd values; the even and odd profiles differ by \((1-2p)^{\tau-1}(\tfrac12-\tfrac{6p}{5})\) in their mass on \(11\), and averaging the resulting distances gives the stated formula. Among the six balanced labelings the RM values are \(\mathrm{RM}_\tau(a\oplus b)\), \(\mathrm{RM}_\tau(a)\), and \(\mathrm{RM}_\tau(b)\), each twice; since \(0<\mathrm{RM}_\tau(b)<\mathrm{RM}_\tau(a)\) for \(0<p<\tfrac12\), the median is \(\mathrm{RM}_\tau(b)\).
\end{proof}

Figure~\ref{fig:parity-robustness} compares the frozen experimental values with these closed forms. The parity lens beats the sampled median at all \(12\) grid points \((p_{\mathrm{leak}},\tau)\). Its frozen RM values are all below \(1.4\times 10^{-16}\), which is floating-point residue of an exact zero; an independent recomputation of all twelve points in rational arithmetic gives exactly \(0\) for parity and exactly \(p_{\mathrm{leak}}|1-2p_{\mathrm{leak}}|^{\tau-1}/10\) for the median, for example \(1/200\) at \(p_{\mathrm{leak}}=0.05,\ \tau=1\) and \(1/50\) at \(p_{\mathrm{leak}}=0.20,\ \tau=1\). The sampled median coincides with the exhaustive median at every grid point. The AND lens is far from closed on the same slices, with RM \(0.293\) and \(0.173\).

The result is useful because parity stays closed even as it becomes a noisy signal. By Proposition~\ref{prop:parity-exact}, the probability that the parity bit has changed after \(\tau\) steps is \([1-(1-2p_{\mathrm{leak}})^\tau]/2\), which grows from \(0.05\) at \(p_{\mathrm{leak}}=0.05,\ \tau=1\) to \(0.4352\) at \(p_{\mathrm{leak}}=0.20,\ \tau=4\), while its RM stays exactly zero. This is the distinction the framework is designed to expose. A coarse variable can become less predictable and still be the right closure variable for the layer: robustness here means that the packaged distinction continues to define a macro-state whose future does not depend on the hidden detail, not that its value is transmitted without error. Figure~\ref{fig:parity-robustness} also shows that the other partitions improve at long horizons only because their mismatch decays like \(|1-2p_{\mathrm{leak}}|^{\tau-1}\) as the chain mixes, not because they become closed.

The contrast with AND is the structural point. AND is not an adversarial choice; it is a familiar Boolean predicate on the same bits. In this laboratory, however, it cuts across the sector structure instead of following it, and its RM exceeds the median balanced partition at every grid point. Semantically familiar predicates are not automatically closure-friendly. A substrate does not inherit its logic from our preferred vocabulary; its dynamics determines which distinctions it can stabilize.

This is the sense of XOR in the title. In this laboratory parity is the packaged distinction that the dynamics itself respects, by construction. That is a designed demonstration, not a theorem that parity dominates other variables in arbitrary substrates. The general lesson is more modest: variables aligned with a system's dominant sector or invariant structure can be exactly closed coarse variables when other natural candidates are not \cite{simonando1961aggregation,shalizi2025macrostate,barnettseth2023dynamical}. The next section asks how far closure extends from a single robust variable to complete gates under noise, staging, and timescale stress.
