\section{Gate Closure Phase Diagrams}
\label{sec:results-phase}

We now move from a single robust variable to complete gates under stress. Logic in these laboratories is not simply present or absent; closure occupies regimes. The frozen phase bundle covers \(108\) conditions: three gates, three staging barriers, three evaluation horizons \(\tau\), and four output-noise levels \(p_{\mathrm{gate}}\). For each condition it records the truth error relative to the intended one-step gate, the conditional entropy \(H(\mathrm{out}\mid\mathrm{in})\), and the route mismatch of the output-bit lens, weighted by the stationary distribution. Figure~\ref{fig:gate-phase} shows all three diagnostics for every condition.

The channels have closed forms, which explains the patterns in the figure. Write \(m=p_{\mathrm{mem}}\), \(g=p_{\mathrm{gate}}\), and let \(q_s=[1-(1-2m)^s]/2\) be the probability that a stored bit has flipped an odd number of times in \(s\) ticks. At the last tick the output is computed from the stored inputs as they were one tick earlier, so for \(\tau\ge1\) the NOT truth error is
\[
\mathrm{err}_{\mathrm{truth}}^{\mathrm{NOT}} = g+q_{\tau-1}-2g\,q_{\tau-1},
\]
and for AND the probability that the output is \(1\), given initial inputs \((a,b)\), is \(g+(1-2g)\,r_a r_b\) with \(r_0=q_{\tau-1}\) and \(r_1=1-q_{\tau-1}\). For CNOT, \(\tau\) ideal applications act as the identity when \(\tau\) is even and as CNOT when \(\tau\) is odd. The control enters the target an odd number of times only when \(\tau\) is odd, and exactly \(\lfloor \tau/2\rfloor\) independent memory flips of the control survive in the target, so the target differs from the \(\tau\)-step ideal map with probability
\[
e_\tau=\tfrac12\bigl[1-(1-2g)^{\tau}(1-2m)^{\lfloor\tau/2\rfloor}\bigr],
\]
independently of the input. The truth error against the one-step XOR table is therefore \(e_\tau\) for odd \(\tau\) and \(\tfrac12\) for even \(\tau\). An independent audit recomputes every metric in all \(108\) conditions from these formulas and recomputes every RM value from rational macro-kernels and explicitly constructed stationary laws, without importing the pipeline's stationary solver or channel code (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-lean}). All conditions agree, and truth error, induced error, and conditional entropy rise monotonically with output noise throughout the grid.

Averaged over barriers and horizons, the NOT truth error rises from \(0.0236\) at \(p_{\mathrm{gate}}=0\) to \(0.1189\) at \(p_{\mathrm{gate}}=0.1\), and the AND truth error from \(0.0225\) to \(0.1180\). The CNOT average rises only from \(0.3333\) to \(0.3667\), because two thirds of its conditions have even \(\tau\), where the error is \(\tfrac12\) by construction. Averages hide most of the structure: barrier and horizon matter about as much as output noise, and the three diagnostics do not move together in the same way for every gate.

\paragraph{NOT and AND: closure fails when storage drifts.}
With strong staging and a short window, both gates are recovered cleanly. At barrier \(6\), \(\tau=1\), and \(p_{\mathrm{gate}}=0\), NOT and AND both have \(\mathrm{err}_{\mathrm{truth}}=0\), \(H(\mathrm{out}\mid\mathrm{in})=0\), and output-lens \(\mathrm{RM}=0.000496\). The small RM is not zero for a structural reason. The output bit alone does not determine its own future, which depends on the stored inputs; in our Lean formalization, the noise-free NOT update provably fails to descend to the output bit on the whole state space. Stationary weighting, however, concentrates almost all mass on states whose output already agrees with the gate, so the average mismatch is small. Under weak staging and longer windows the picture changes even without output noise, because the stored inputs themselves drift. At barrier \(0\) (\(p_{\mathrm{mem}}=0.05\)), \(\tau=4\), and \(p_{\mathrm{gate}}=0\), NOT reaches truth error \(0.1355=q_3\), entropy \(0.572\), and RM \(0.139\), and AND reaches truth error \(0.126\), entropy \(0.497\), and RM \(0.142\). The symbolic gate has not changed; the carrier has become unreliable, and with it the closure.

\paragraph{CNOT: a perfect channel on an unclosed bit.}
CNOT behaves differently because its output lens reads the target bit alone. At \(\tau=1\) and \(p_{\mathrm{gate}}=0\), CNOT is an exact gate channel at every barrier: \(\mathrm{err}_{\mathrm{truth}}=0\) and \(H(\mathrm{out}\mid\mathrm{in})=0\). Yet the output-lens RM is \(1\), the largest value RM can take for a two-label lens: two microstates with the same target bit but different controls have opposite futures. At \(\tau=1\) with output noise, the RM is exactly \(|1-2p_{\mathrm{gate}}|\) at every barrier. This is not a contradiction. A perfect input--output channel says that the full input determines the output; closure says that the output label determines its own future. The Lean formalization proves that the one-step CNOT update admits no induced map on the target bit alone. The reversible two-bit gate is closed on its full register, while the one-bit output lens is not.

At even horizons the roles reverse. Two ideal applications return the target to its initial value, so the one-step XOR table is the wrong reference and the truth error is \(\tfrac12\) by construction. The induced error, which grades the channel against its own best table, stays small at barrier \(6\) and \(p_{\mathrm{gate}}=0\): \(0.000124\) at \(\tau=2\) and \(0.000248\) at \(\tau=4\), with conditional entropy \(0.0018\) and \(0.0033\). The output-lens RM is exactly \(0\) at every even horizon, barrier, and noise level, because \(e_\tau\) does not depend on the input: after an even number of ticks the target is its initial value plus independent noise, and so it does determine its own future. Lean proves the noise-free version of this statement, that the two-tick CNOT update descends to the target bit. The \(\tau>1\) rows of CNOT are therefore closure stress tests, not repeated grading of one truth table: the relevant macro-operator changes with the horizon.

Read as a whole, Figure~\ref{fig:gate-phase} is a phase portrait of embodied logic. Output noise raises channel error in the expected way, but staging and horizon decide whether that noise is the dominant failure or one contributor among several. For NOT and AND the central transition runs from a short-horizon, high-barrier regime in which the gate is closed in practice to a long-horizon, weak-staging regime in which memory drift drives both entropy and mismatch up. For CNOT the lesson is that closure depends on retaining the right carrier: a reversible gate can be channel-perfect at one horizon while its one-bit output lens is maximally unclosed.
