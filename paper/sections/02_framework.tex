\section{Logic Layers, Packaging, and Closure}
\label{sec:framework}

We do not rederive the full Six Birds formalism here \cite{tsiokos2026sixbirds}. Four ingredients are enough to talk about logic: a lens that fixes what the layer can observe, a packaging step that turns observations into reusable binary carriers, an induced dynamics that may or may not close on those carriers, and an audit that measures persistence, mismatch, and loss. In Six Birds terms, packaging (P5) supplies the carriers, constraints (P2) give them meaning, sectors and metastability (P4) keep them stable, operator rewrite (P1) governs which effective operators can be introduced, protocol holonomy (P3) shows up as dependence on the order of evaluation, and accounting (P6) keeps the description honest. This section turns these ingredients into a definition of a \emph{logic layer}.

\subsection{Lenses, definability, and packaged predicates}

Let \(Z\) be a finite set of microstates and let
\[
f: Z \to X
\]
be a lens, or coarse-graining map, into a set of labels \(X\). The fibers \(f^{-1}(x)\) are the groups of microstates that the layer does not tell apart. A binary predicate \(h: Z \to \{0,1\}\) is \emph{definable} at the lens if it factors through it, that is, if
\[
h = \tilde h \circ f
\]
for some macro-predicate \(\tilde h : X \to \{0,1\}\). This holds exactly when \(h\) is constant on every fiber of \(f\). The equivalence needs no assumption that \(f\) is onto; when it is not, \(\tilde h\) is determined on the image of \(f\) and arbitrary elsewhere. We write \(\Sigma_f\) for the collection of definable predicates, or equivalently of definable events \(A \subseteq Z\) (unions of fibers). \(\Sigma_f\) is the expressive content of the layer: a distinction that is not definable at \(f\) is not available as a statement at that layer \cite{pawlak1982rough,ellerman2010partitions,shalizi2025macrostate}.

Two consequences are immediate, and both are proved in our Lean formalization (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-lean}). First, \(\Sigma_f\) contains the always-true and always-false predicates and is closed under conjunction, disjunction, negation, and exclusive or; it is a Boolean algebra. Second, definability is inherited along any update that descends to the labels: if \(F: Z\to Z\) satisfies \(f\circ F = g\circ f\) for some \(g: X \to X\), then \(h\circ F\) is definable whenever \(h\) is.

Packaging is the step that turns a definable distinction into a reusable carrier. In the binary case a package is a map \(\Pi : Z \to \{0,1\}\) that assigns many microstates to the same label because the layer treats them as equivalent. For a definable event \(A\), the packaged predicate is its indicator \(\Pi_A(z) = \mathbf{1}[z \in A]\). Truth values are thus compressed labels of microstates rather than primitive objects. They are useful only if they persist over the timescale on which the layer operates, which is where sectors and metastability (P4) enter \cite{simonando1961aggregation,deuflhardweber2005pcca,shalizi2025macrostate}.

\subsection{Two complementary views of logic}

In the \emph{feasibility view}, propositions are definable sets of admissible microstates and connectives are set operations on them:
\[
A \wedge B = A \cap B,\qquad
A \vee B = A \cup B,\qquad
\neg A = Z \setminus A,\qquad
A \oplus B = (A \setminus B) \cup (B \setminus A).
\]
Logic in this view is not an extra layer placed on top of the dynamics. It is the algebra of distinctions that a lens can express. Constraints (P2) appear as admissibility: a proposition restricts which states are allowed, and a contradiction is the empty set. Because \(\Sigma_f\) is a Boolean algebra, every connective of definable events is again definable. In particular, the complement of a definable event is always available \emph{as a statement}. Whether the substrate can \emph{execute} negation, that is, whether some dynamics maps each state carrying a bit to a state carrying its complement, is a separate operational question.

In the \emph{operator view}, a packaged bit matters only if the dynamics induces updates on it. For a deterministic micro-update \(F: Z \to Z\), an induced macro-update \(g: X\to X\) exists if and only if \(F\) respects the fibers, meaning that \(f(z)=f(z')\) implies \(f(F(z))=f(F(z'))\). When it exists, \(g\) is unique on the image of \(f\), and induced updates compose: the update induced by \(F_2\circ F_1\) is the composite of the updates induced by \(F_1\) and \(F_2\). For stochastic dynamics given by a Markov kernel \(P\) on \(Z\), the corresponding requirement is lumpability \cite{kemeny1976finite,buchholz1994lumpability,simonando1961aggregation,givon2004extracting}. Write
\[
R_\tau(i,x) = \sum_{j:\,f(j)=x} (P^\tau)_{ij}
\]
for the probability that microstate \(i\) reaches label \(x\) after \(\tau\) steps. The lens is lumpable at horizon \(\tau\) when \(R_\tau(i,\cdot)\) is the same for all \(i\) in each fiber; the common rows then form a macro-kernel \(K_{\tau,f}\) on the image \(f(Z)\), and evolving and then coarse-graining agrees with coarse-graining and then evolving:
\[
Q_f(\mu P^\tau) = Q_f(\mu)\,K_{\tau,f}\qquad\text{for every distribution } \mu \text{ on } Z,
\]
where \(Q_f\) pushes a distribution forward through the lens. Section~\ref{sec:diagnostics-methods} measures how far a lens is from this condition.

It is also useful to have a map that goes back from labels to microstates. Fix for each nonempty fiber a probability vector supported on that fiber (its \emph{prototype}; we use the uniform one) and let \(U_f\) send a macro-distribution supported on \(f(Z)\) to the corresponding mixture of prototypes. Then \(Q_f U_f\) is the identity on macro-distributions supported on the image of \(f\), and the packaging map \(T=U_f Q_f\) is idempotent: packaging twice is the same as packaging once. The dynamical map
\[
E_{\tau,f}(\mu) = U_f\bigl(Q_f(\mu P^\tau)\bigr)
\]
evolves the micro-distribution, reduces it through the lens, and lifts the result back to a packaged micro-distribution. Unlike \(T\), the map \(E_{\tau,f}\) is in general not idempotent.

The two views need each other. Feasibility without operator closure gives semantics that the substrate cannot execute. Operator closure without feasibility semantics gives a convenient signal-processing description, but not yet a logic. A logic layer appears when the same packaged distinctions support both.

\begin{definition}[Logic layer]
\label{def:logic-layer}
Fix a microstate space \(Z\), a lens \(f : Z \to X\), and an evaluation window \(\tau\). A \emph{logic layer} at \((f,\tau)\) is a nontrivial family \(\mathcal{B} \subseteq \Sigma_f\) of binary packaged predicates, together with induced macro-updates, such that:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stability.} Each \(b \in \mathcal{B}\) is metastable on the timescale \(\tau\).
    \item \textbf{Closure.} The relevant micro-updates descend to macro-updates on the packaged variables with small closure defect.
    \item \textbf{Feasibility semantics.} Each \(b \in \mathcal{B}\) is the indicator of a definable feasible set, and the connectives the layer uses are definable and stable at \((f,\tau)\).
    \item \textbf{Auditability.} Persistence, agreement between evaluation routes, and information loss are measured by explicit diagnostics.
\end{enumerate}
When these conditions fail, a symbolic description may still be convenient, but it is not a closed logic layer in the sense of this paper.
\end{definition}

Definition~\ref{def:logic-layer} is an operational criterion, not a theorem. It does not fix numerical thresholds for ``metastable'' or ``small''; those depend on the application, and the experiments below report the diagnostics themselves rather than a single pass/fail verdict. In the set-based laboratories of this paper, clause~3 adds little beyond clause~1. Definability of the connectives is automatic, and their stability is controlled by that of their inputs: in the stability measure \(S_\tau\) of Section~\ref{sec:diagnostics-methods}, a predicate and its complement are equally stable, and a connective of two predicates \(b\) and \(c\) can change value only if \(b\) or \(c\) does, so
\[
S_\tau(b \star c) \;\ge\; 1-\bigl(1-S_\tau(b)\bigr)-\bigl(1-S_\tau(c)\bigr)
\]
for any binary connective \(\star\). The clause carries more weight in settings where admissible states or executable operations are restricted.

\subsection{Why the criterion is layer-relative}

Definition~\ref{def:logic-layer} asks for more than the ability to label states by zero and one. A candidate bit must last long enough to matter, must support induced operators, and must survive audit. This is why the six primitives work together rather than overlap: P5 packages the carrier, P4 keeps it from dissolving, P2 makes it meaningful, P1 determines whether an effective operator vocabulary can be stabilized, P3 detects dependence on evaluation order and hidden protocol state, and P6 records what it costs to maintain the arrangement.

The criterion is also deliberately broader than classical Booleanity. In the laboratories studied here the expressible events always form a Boolean algebra, so what varies from layer to layer is not whether complements exist as statements but which operations the dynamics carries out reliably, at which horizon, and on which carrier. A layer may support a stable parity bit but no reliable conjunction, or may support a reversible operator only on a larger carrier than the one being read. We therefore speak of a \emph{logic layer} rather than a \emph{Boolean layer}. Genuinely non-Boolean event structures, of the kind studied in quantum logic and partition logic \cite{birkhoff1936quantumlogic,ellerman2010partitions,atmanspacher2015nonboolean}, would require restricting the admissible operations or enriching the semantics, which the present laboratories do not do.

Section~\ref{sec:diagnostics-methods} turns the four clauses into measurements: predicate stability for the first clause, route mismatch and closure defect for the second, feasible-set and gate semantics for the third, and unretained input information and entropy production for the fourth.
